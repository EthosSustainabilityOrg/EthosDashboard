# **Purpose**

The Chapter Lead Training Guide provides chapter leaders with an overview of the systems, responsibilities, and resources they use to operate an Ethos Sustainability chapter.

Chapter leads receive training on these responsibilities, but this document serves as an ongoing reference for information they may need after training.

The goal is to help chapter leaders understand where information is located, how communication works, how chapter activities are managed, and where to go when additional support is needed.

---

# **Role of a Chapter Lead**

The Chapter Lead is responsible for helping the chapter remain active, organized, and connected to Ethos Sustainability Headquarters.

The Chapter Lead generally serves as the primary connection between:

* The chapter.  
* Chapter officers.  
* General members.  
* Faculty Advisor.  
* Ethos Sustainability Headquarters.

The Chapter Lead may also be responsible for coordinating with other officers to ensure that chapter requirements are completed.

Depending on the chapter's bylaws, the Chapter Lead may be the President or another designated officer.

---

# **Chapter Lead Responsibilities**

Chapter leadership responsibilities generally include:

* Maintaining communication with HQ.  
* Participating in monthly Chapter Leads Meetings.  
* Coordinating chapter activities.  
* Supporting at least one event or activity each month.  
* Ensuring required Project Proposals are submitted.  
* Following financial approval requirements.  
* Supporting completion of Post-Event Reports.  
* Supporting the Semester Impact Document.  
* Maintaining communication with the Faculty Advisor.  
* Supporting member recruitment.  
* Following Ethos Sustainability policies.  
* Helping officers understand their responsibilities.  
* Asking HQ for clarification when necessary.

Chapter leads are not expected to handle every responsibility themselves. Effective chapters distribute responsibilities among their officers and members.

---

# **Understanding Chapter Leadership**

Ethos Sustainability chapters operate as part of the larger organization.

This means chapters have the ability to:

* Organize local activities.  
* Recruit members.  
* Develop sustainability projects.  
* Conduct educational programs.  
* Build appropriate community relationships.  
* Participate in HQ events.

At the same time, certain areas remain under HQ oversight, including applicable:

* Financial decisions.  
* Major project approvals.  
* Legal documentation.  
* Organizational branding.  
* Major partnerships.  
* Sponsorship arrangements.  
* Safety requirements.

The specific responsibilities of HQ and chapter leadership are described throughout the Chapter Starter Kit.

# **Chapter Communication Systems**

Ethos Sustainability uses several communication systems.

The primary systems identified for chapter operations are:

### **Slack**

Used primarily for communication between officers and HQ staff.

Slack may be used for:

* Operations.  
* Coordination.  
* Questions.  
* Approvals.  
* Chapter updates.  
* Program discussions.

### **Monthly Google Meet**

Used for regular communication between chapter leads and HQ.

The meeting provides:

* HQ updates.  
* Chapter updates.  
* Upcoming event information.  
* Questions and discussion.  
* Coordination between chapter leaders.

### **Ethos Sustainability Home Website / App**

Used as a central resource for chapter and organizational information.

Depending on the system's available features, chapter leads may use it to access:

* Chapter information.  
* Contact information.  
* Event information.  
* Communication tools.  
* Training resources.  
* Organizational resources.  
* Proposal and reporting information.

The specific features available may change as the platform develops.

# **Tools and Authentication**

Chapter leaders may receive access to organizational tools based on their role.

Access may include:

* Slack.  
* Google Workspace resources.  
* Ethos Sustainability Home Website / App.  
* Chapter-specific resources.

If access is lost, credentials do not work, or a chapter leader believes an account has been compromised, the issue should be reported to the appropriate HQ contact.

# **Submitting Proposals and Reports**

Chapter activities may require documents to be submitted to HQ.

Examples include:

### **Before an Event**

A Project Proposal may be required when a chapter is planning a project or event.

The proposal may include:

* Purpose.  
* Learning goals.  
* Audience.  
* Activities.  
* Budget.  
* Funding plan.  
* Timeline.  
* Staffing.  
* Safety considerations.

### **After an Event**

A Post-Event Report may be required.

The report may include:

* Attendance.  
* Outcomes.  
* Participant feedback.  
* Financial information.  
* Photos with appropriate consent.  
* Challenges.  
* Support requests.

### **At the End of a Semester**

A Semester Impact Document is submitted to summarize the chapter's work and impact.

Chapter leaders should use the approved templates and submission processes provided by HQ.

# **Working With the Faculty Advisor**

The Faculty Advisor is an important connection between the chapter and its school or institution.

The advisor may support:

* School compliance.  
* Event oversight.  
* Student safety.  
* School communication.  
* Chapter continuity.

The Chapter Lead should maintain appropriate communication with the Faculty Advisor and involve them when school requirements or student safety are relevant.

# **Working With Chapter Officers**

Chapter leads should work with the officer team rather than managing the chapter alone.

Officer responsibilities may include:

* President / Chapter Lead.  
* Vice President.  
* Secretary.  
* Treasurer.  
* Outreach Coordinator.  
* Optional program or media roles.

Each officer should understand their responsibilities and communicate with the rest of the leadership team.

# **Active Chapter Expectations**

Chapter leaders help ensure that the chapter remains active.

Core expectations include:

* Participating in monthly Chapter Leads Meetings.  
* Conducting at least one event or activity each month.  
* Completing required Project Proposals.  
* Completing required Post-Event Reports.  
* Completing the Semester Impact Document.  
* Following HQ approval requirements.  
* Maintaining good standing with the school and HQ.  
* Following the organization's non-discrimination expectations.

---

# **When to Contact HQ**

Chapter leaders should contact HQ whenever they are unsure about an organizational requirement.

Examples include questions about:

* Event approval.  
* Finances.  
* Sponsorships.  
* Partnerships.  
* Legal documents.  
* Safety.  
* Branding.  
* Media.  
* Major projects.  
* Organizational policy.  
* Unusual circumstances.

Chapter leaders should not assume that an activity is approved simply because a similar activity happened previously.

---

# **Asking Questions**

Questions are an expected part of chapter leadership.

When asking HQ for help, it is useful to provide:

* The chapter name.  
* The event or project involved.  
* The relevant date.  
* What the chapter is trying to accomplish.  
* The specific question or concern.  
* Any relevant documents or deadlines.

Providing this information helps HQ respond efficiently.

---

# **Chapter Lead Continuity**

Chapter leadership may change from year to year.

Outgoing chapter leaders should help ensure that important information is transferred to incoming leaders.

This may include:

* Chapter documents.  
* Event information.  
* Contact information.  
* Current projects.  
* Important deadlines.  
* Approved resources.  
* Access to organizational systems.

Organizational accounts and resources should be transferred according to HQ procedures rather than through informal personal account sharing.

---

# **Leadership and Organizational Conduct**

Chapter leaders represent Ethos Sustainability when acting on behalf of the organization.

Leaders are expected to:

* Communicate professionally.  
* Treat others respectfully.  
* Follow organizational policies.  
* Avoid discrimination.  
* Protect confidential or private information.  
* Use organizational resources appropriately.  
* Seek approval when required.  
* Communicate honestly about chapter activities.

# **Key Considerations**

### **You Do Not Have to Know Everything**

Chapter leads are not expected to know every organizational policy or process from memory. The Chapter Starter Kit and HQ support are available as resources.

### **Use the Right System**

Slack, the website/app, Google Meet, and other tools have different purposes. Use the appropriate platform for the information being communicated.

### **Keep HQ Informed**

Regular communication helps HQ support chapters and identify problems early.

### **Use Current Documents**

Organizational procedures and forms may change. Use the current version available through the approved Ethos Sustainability resource system.

### **Ask Before Acting When Unsure**

If a decision involves money, legal documents, safety, major partnerships, branding, or another area requiring HQ approval, ask before proceeding.

### **Leadership Is a Team Responsibility**

The Chapter Lead coordinates the chapter but does not need to complete every task personally. Officers and members should contribute according to their roles.
