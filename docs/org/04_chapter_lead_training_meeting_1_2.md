---

# **2\. What Is Ethos Sustainability? — 10 minutes**

### **Highlights**

**What is Ethos?**

* Nonprofit organization.  
* Focuses on sustainability, education, entrepreneurship, business, and STEAM.  
* Creates opportunities for students and communities.

**Why do chapters exist?**

* Bring Ethos programming into local schools and communities.  
* Give members opportunities to participate and lead.  
* Create local sustainability projects and events.  
* Expand the impact of Ethos.

**How does a chapter fit into Ethos?**

**HQ → Chapter Leadership → Officers → Members → Community**

Explain that chapters have independence to create ideas while still operating within Ethos expectations and approval processes.

---

# **3\. What Does a Chapter Lead Actually Do? — 10 minutes**

### **Highlights**

A Chapter Lead is responsible for helping the chapter:

* Stay organized.  
* Communicate with HQ.  
* Coordinate officers.  
* Keep the chapter active.  
* Plan events and projects.  
* Make sure required documents are completed.  
* Communicate important information to members.  
* Follow Ethos policies and approval requirements.

### **Important Clarification**

A Chapter Lead **doesn't have to do everything themselves.**

Their job is to help the team function.

For example:

> Instead of the Chapter Lead designing every Instagram post, the Communications Officer can handle media while the Chapter Lead coordinates the overall project.

# **4\. Understanding Chapter Leadership — 10 minutes**

Use the **Officer Roles** document here.

### **Highlights**

Explain:

* Why chapters have multiple leadership positions.  
* What each officer is responsible for.  
* How responsibilities are divided.  
* Why communication between officers matters.  
* How officers support the Chapter Lead.

### **Quick Activity**

Give them a hypothetical event:

> "Your chapter is hosting a sustainability workshop."

Ask:

**Who handles what?**

Have them identify which officer would likely handle:

* Budget  
* Promotion  
* Attendance  
* Event planning  
* Member communication  
* Documentation

This makes the roles practical instead of just reading descriptions.

# **6\. HQ vs. Chapter — 10 minutes**

### **Chapter Responsibilities**

Chapters can:

* Develop ideas.  
* Plan local activities.  
* Recruit members.  
* Organize approved events.  
* Build relationships within their community.  
* Lead chapter activities.

### **HQ Responsibilities**

HQ provides:

* Organizational direction.  
* Support.  
* Policies.  
* Resources.  
* Approval where required.  
* Organization-wide coordination.

**Chapters have room to create—but they don't operate completely independently.**

Use a few examples:

| Situation | Chapter Can Do It Alone? |
| ----- | ----- |
| Brainstorm an event idea | Yes  |
| Plan a member meeting | Yes  |
| Create a chapter activity | Depends on activity |
| Spend chapter funds | Follow financial policy |
| Major sponsorship | No, HQ approval required |
| Formal partnership | Follow HQ approval |
| Use certain official media/branding | Follow HQ requirements |

**This gives them a practical understanding of where the line is.**

# **7\. BUILD: Chapter Foundation — 20 minutes**

Now they begin their **Chapter Action & Launch Plan**.

Have each chapter lead start a shared document/template.

### **Part 1 — Chapter Identity**

They fill in:

**Chapter Name:**  
\[School / Community\]

**Chapter Lead:**  
\[Name\]

**Officers:**  
\[Names \+ Positions\]

---

### **Part 2 — Chapter Purpose**

Ask:

> **Why does your chapter exist?**

They write 2–3 sentences explaining what they want their chapter to accomplish.

---

### **Part 3 — Who Are We Serving?**

They identify:

* Primary audience.  
* Age/grade range.  
* School/community.  
* Interests or needs.

---

### **Part 4 — Chapter Goals**

Have them create **3–5 initial goals**.

For example:

* Build an active membership.  
* Host monthly sustainability activities.  
* Introduce students to sustainable entrepreneurship.  
* Complete a community sustainability project.

The goals don't have to be perfect yet.

**They will be refined throughout the training.**

---

# **8\. Chapter Vision Activity — 10 minutes**

Ask each chapter:

> **"If your chapter is extremely successful one year from now, what does it look like?"**

Have them discuss:

* How many active members?  
* What types of events?  
* What impact?  
* What does the community know the chapter for?  
* What would members say they gained?

Then have them write a short **Chapter Vision Statement**.

---

# **9\. Build Check-In — 5 minutes**

Have everyone look at what they've created.

Their Chapter Action & Launch Plan should now contain:

Chapter identity  
Leadership structure  
Chapter purpose  
Target audience  
Initial goals  
Vision statement

Explain:

> "This is only the beginning. Every week, we're going to add another section."
